American Express: durable trust and network economics

Thesis reviewed 2026-10-05 · Documented investor thesis

Why the investor bought — what the evidence supports

Buffett's 2023 shareholder letter describes American Express's globally recognizable brand and the timeless need for unquestioned financial trust. He emphasizes staying with a wonderful business as earnings and dividends grow. Berkshire's Q2 2026 share count is unchanged, with 17.14% portfolio weight: conviction, not new buying.

Our thesis

The affluent-customer brand, merchant network and membership model can sustain returns above ordinary banking. AXP remains the quality payments benchmark rather than forcing a weaker small-cap substitute.

Valuation cross-check

15.1x analyst forward earnings and 34.4% vendor ROE. Ordinary FCF yield is omitted because lending, deposits and regulatory capital make industrial-company FCF comparisons misleading. A 25% cut to forecast EPS gives 20.1x.

Where the thesis or analogy breaks

Credit losses, spending slowdowns, rewards costs and regulation can overwhelm fee growth. ROE must be read alongside capital and leverage, not as a standalone moat measure.

What must be established next

Check through-cycle credit losses, capital needs and membership/merchant retention. WEX is a niche payments analogue, not a replacement for AXP's brand or network.

Executive summary

American Express Company, together with its subsidiaries, operates as an integrated payments company in the United States, Europe, the Middle East and Africa, the Asia Pacific, Australia, New Zealand, Latin America, Canada, the Caribbean, and internationally.

It operates through four segments: U.S.

Consumer Services, Commercial Services, International Card Services, and Global Merchant and Network Services.

The company offers credit and charge cards and complementary products and services, including travel, dining, and lifestyle and expense management products and services; and banking and other payment and financing products and services, including deposits and non-card lending.

Business model and why it may be working

  • Makes money within the Credit Services value chain, based on the operating profile described by company filings and market data.
  • Recent growth is being helped by revenue expanding about 12.8% year over year.
  • Its edge appears to come from a high-margin business model, which usually signals pricing power, IP, or a niche position.
