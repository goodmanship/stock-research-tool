Alphabet (GOOG / GOOGL): the quality anchor

Thesis reviewed 2026-10-05 · Documented investor rationale, with our business interpretation

Why the investor bought — what the evidence supports

Buffett told CNBC in July 2026 that he initiated the position and emphasized durable high returns on capital and real distributable cash. He also explicitly acknowledged the enormous AI capital-spending burden. This documents his framework, not a disclosed price target or a full search/AI valuation model. Li Lu's two share classes total 47.94% of his Q2 disclosed portfolio; that is one underlying business, not two investments.

Our thesis

Search intent, YouTube distribution and Cloud can support durable cash generation. Keep Alphabet as the large-company quality benchmark; GOOG and GOOGL have the same underlying economics, with different voting rights and slightly different quotes.

Valuation cross-check

At the reviewed snapshot: 22.9x analyst forward earnings; vendor FCF yield only 0.54%. Annual-statement FCF was $73.3B in 2025, or 1.73% of today's market cap; the 2023–25 average gives 1.70%. Subtracting 2025 stock compensation gives a rough 1.14% owner-cash proxy. None establishes a cash-flow bargain. A 25% cut to forecast EPS raises forward P/E to 30.6x.

Where the thesis or analogy breaks

AI can weaken search monetization while requiring more capital; antitrust remedies and distribution costs can erode the moat. High historical ROE does not establish the return on the next dollar spent on AI.

What must be established next

Estimate sustainable cash after maintenance and growth capex, then test whether incremental AI/Cloud returns justify the current valuation. Priority business research, not an unconditional buy.

offers various products and platforms in the United States, Europe, the Middle East, Africa, the Asia-Pacific, Canada, and Latin America.
