How the thesis search works

  1. Verify ownership and accumulation. Use Dataroma filing-quarter holdings and share-count histories. Preferred investors Buffett/Berkshire, Li Lu, Pabrai and Klarman receive extra screening weight; a trim is not a buying signal.
  2. Find the actual reason. Investor letters, interviews and presentations can document it. A 13F cannot. Here, Alphabet/AXP and DECK/CROX have documented rationale; GPC/LEN mappings are explicitly our inference. No Li Lu stock-specific CROX explanation was verified.
  3. Extract the economics. Durable customer behavior, pricing power, recurring demand, capital requirements and reinvestment runway matter more than sharing an industry label.
  4. Compare size and valuation. Check forward earnings, cash after capex, returns and balance sheets alongside the anchor; retain reasons the analogy fails.
  5. Normalize before upgrading. Separate growth and maintenance capex; account for stock compensation, cycle peaks, insurer/bank funding and one-off refunds. These remain research ideas until the valuation work clears that hurdle.

Historical annual-statement cash-flow checks use Yahoo's standardized cash-flow statements (fiscal dates stated in the notes), calculated as operating cash less reported capex and divided by the October 5 market cap. They are cross-checks, not independently audited extracts or fair-value forecasts. Vendor info-field FCF, annual FCF, company guidance and half-year results are kept explicitly labeled. FCF less stock compensation is only a rough dilution-cost sensitivity. Thesis text and these historical checks remain dated {{ research.reviewed }} when market quotes refresh; a new quote does not constitute a new thesis review.
